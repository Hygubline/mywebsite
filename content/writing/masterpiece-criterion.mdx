If we are to discuss what truly qualifies as a masterpiece, there is a stark and rather unforgiving criterion we might apply: after a work concludes, are people still discussing its characters, or have they begun debating the world itself?

By this standard, I am more inclined to regard *Attack on Titan* as closer to a "masterpiece."

## The Difference in Gravity

When *Code Geass* is mentioned, discussions tend to revolve around Lelouch as an individual—his character, his choices, and his ending. When *Attack on Titan* is brought up, the conversation often expands outward into war, nationalism, freedom, and the cyclical nature of hatred.

This is not a matter of one being "deeper" than the other; it is a difference in narrative focus.

- One centers its gravity on the individual.
- The other places it upon the structure of the world.

**One tells the story of how an individual might save the world.**
**The other compels us to confront why the world may be unsalvageable.**

## Romanticism vs. Existential Dilemma

At its core, *Code Geass* represents romanticism in its most distilled form:

> The world can be corrected, if only one person is willing to bear the burden.
> Evil can be quantified, centralized, and eliminated in a single decisive act.

But when the boundaries of the world are finally unveiled in *Attack on Titan*, true freedom does not arrive. Instead, the series reveals an existential dilemma:

> The world is not broken because of a single villain; the tragedy is structural.
> Every choice inevitably generates new sacrifices.

In its later stages, the narrative repeatedly poses a question that admits no definitive answer:

*Was it Marley that gave rise to Eldia's tragedy?*
*Or was it Eldia that sowed the seeds of Marley's catastrophe?*

## Youth and Adulthood

*Code Geass* embodies what we longed to become in our youth: someone powerful enough to shoulder sin, reshape the world, and exit the stage in a blaze of tragic grandeur.

*Attack on Titan* reflects the condition we must confront as adults: striving with all our strength, only to discover a world constrained by forces beyond individual will—where there are no clean solutions, only compromises made in the mud of reality.

## In Defense of Romanticism

This is not to diminish the greatness of *Code Geass*. On the contrary, its power lies precisely in its romantic conviction. It moves us because belief itself is beautiful.

---

If *Code Geass* is a work that invites us to believe the world can still be designed,
then *Attack on Titan* is one that helps us understand why the world resists design.

In that sense, it stands closer to the realm of masterpiece.
