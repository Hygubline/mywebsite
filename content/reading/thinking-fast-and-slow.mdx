The core frame is simple and sticky: the mind runs two systems. **System 1** is fast, automatic, intuitive. **System 2** is slow, deliberate, effortful. Most of the day, System 1 drives, and System 2 only wakes up when things get hard — or when we force it to.

## What stuck with me

- **Confidence is not accuracy.** The feeling of being right is generated by System 1's ease of constructing a story, not by the quality of the evidence.
- **What you see is all there is (WYSIATI).** We build coherent stories from whatever information is in front of us and barely register what's missing.
- **Anchoring is everywhere.** A random number you just saw quietly shifts your next estimate. Knowing this doesn't make you immune.

## How I want to use it

When a decision feels obvious, that's exactly the moment to slow down and ask System 2 to check the work. Especially for things that are hard to reverse.

> A reliable way to make people believe in falsehoods is frequent repetition, because familiarity is not easily distinguished from truth.
