Every time I'm about to publish something — a note, a small project, even this site — there's a little voice that says *maybe not yet.* Maybe when it's better. Maybe when it's finished. Maybe when I'm sure.

## The fear is about being seen, not being wrong

I used to think I was afraid of being wrong. But I'll happily be wrong in a notebook nobody reads. The fear only shows up when the work might be *seen*.

Which means it's not really about the work. It's about me. Publishing turns a private thing into evidence — here is what I find interesting, here is the level I'm at, here is what I think is good.

## Lowering the stakes on purpose

The thing that helps is making it smaller. A polished portfolio is terrifying to publish. A garden of half-finished notes is not — because the whole premise is that it's unfinished.

> If the thing is allowed to be in progress, there's nothing to be caught not-finishing.

So I built a place where everything is openly a work in progress. It's a bit of a trick I'm playing on myself, and it's working.

## The quiet upside

The scary part and the good part are the same part. Being seen is the risk. Being seen is also the only way the work ever connects with anyone. You don't get one without the other.

So: publish the small thing. Then the next one is a little less scary.
