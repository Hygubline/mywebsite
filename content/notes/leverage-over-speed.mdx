Most builders optimize for speed. Ship fast. Iterate faster. Move move move.

But speed without direction is just expensive noise.

## The Leverage Question

Every decision should pass through one filter: does this create leverage?

Leverage means your outputs exceed your inputs. It means one hour of work today saves ten hours tomorrow. It means building systems that compound.

Speed is about velocity. Leverage is about trajectory.

## The Trap of Productivity Theater

We've confused activity with progress. Shipping features with solving problems. Being busy with being effective.

The most dangerous form of this is *productive procrastination* — doing real work that doesn't matter.

- Refactoring code that no one uses
- Building features no one asked for
- Optimizing workflows that shouldn't exist

## A Better Question

Instead of "how can I move faster?", ask:

> What's the smallest thing I can build that creates the most leverage?

This reframes everything. It's not about doing more. It's about doing the right thing.

## The Compounding Effect

Leverage compounds. Speed depletes.

When you build for leverage:
- Each project strengthens the next
- Your skills stack on each other
- Your reputation accumulates

When you build for speed:
- You're always starting from zero
- You burn out chasing the next thing
- Nothing compounds

Choose leverage. Always.
