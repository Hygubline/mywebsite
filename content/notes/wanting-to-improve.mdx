There's a feeling I don't have a clean word for. It's the low hum of wanting to be better — at building, at writing, at thinking, at being a person. It's mostly a gift. It's also exhausting.

## The double edge

The wanting is what gets me to learn things. Without it I'd coast. But the exact same wanting is what makes it hard to sit still and feel okay about today's version of me. The engine and the discontent are the same machine.

## Maybe that's allowed

For a while I tried to fix this — to want improvement *and* feel perfectly at peace. I don't think those fully coexist, and I've stopped treating it as a bug.

> You can be grateful for where you are and hungry for where you're going. The two don't cancel; they just both sit there, humming.

## Pointing it somewhere

What helps isn't silencing the wanting — it's *aiming* it. Vague "be better" is corrosive. Specific "learn this one thing this month" is fuel.

- Turn the restlessness into a concrete, finishable task.
- Let finishing it count, even for an afternoon.
- Then let the hum come back. It always does.

This note is mostly me making peace with a feeling I used to think I had to outgrow.
