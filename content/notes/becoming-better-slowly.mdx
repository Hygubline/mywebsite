I want to get better quickly. Everyone does. But almost everything I'm actually proud of came from getting better *slowly* — so slowly I couldn't feel it happening.

## You can't feel the gradient

The problem with slow improvement is that it's invisible at the resolution of a single day. You practice, and tomorrow you're roughly as good as today. The needle doesn't move enough to register, so it feels like nothing is happening.

But stack 200 of those invisible days and you're a different person. The progress was real the whole time; it was just below the threshold of what a day can show you.

## Compounding rewards consistency, not intensity

A heroic week is satisfying and mostly forgettable. A boring habit kept for a year quietly rearranges who you are.

- The thing you do *most days* beats the thing you do *hard once*.
- Reduce the size of the task until skipping it feels sillier than doing it.
- Measure in months, not afternoons.

> Trust the slope, not the day.

## A gentler relationship with the work

Becoming better slowly also means being allowed to be bad at things on the way. Patience with the gradient is really just patience with yourself.

That's most of what this site is for — a place to keep showing up, slowly.
