There's a strange hollowness I feel sometimes after using AI to solve a problem. The thing works. The bug is gone. The essay is drafted. And yet I close the laptop feeling like I didn't actually *learn* anything.

## Friction is where the learning lives

For a long time I thought the struggle was the inefficient part — the tax you paid before you got to the answer. Now I'm not so sure. The struggle *is* the learning. The dead ends, the re-reading, the moment where it finally clicks — that's not overhead. That's the whole thing.

When I hand the friction to a tool, I get the answer without the residue the friction leaves behind.

## It's not the tool, it's the posture

But I don't think the answer is to stop using AI. That feels like refusing a calculator to prove you can do long division.

The difference seems to be *posture*. When I use it like a vending machine — question in, answer out — I learn nothing. When I use it like a sparring partner — argue with it, ask why, try to predict its answer before I read it — something sticks.

> The goal isn't to avoid the shortcut. It's to stay awake while taking it.

## What I'm trying

- Predict the answer before I ask, then compare.
- Ask it to explain, then explain it back without looking.
- Keep a note of things I *would* have learned the slow way, and learn a few of them anyway.

I haven't solved this. But naming the hollow feeling helped me notice when it shows up.
